    The wizard hides the terrain. This is the terrain.

    • Suspected breach — something built without permission, or conditions being ignored?
      • Yes — someone says the rules are being broken → Planning or licensing?
        • Planning → Find out what, if anything, was actually permitted
          • then → Put the approved drawings next to what's on the ground. What do you have?
            • A permission exists, and what's being built departs from it → Departure from an approved permission — the strongest version of this story
            • A permission exists, and the building matches it → The building matches — so read the conditions, not the drawings
            • Nothing on the portal at all → No digital shortcut exists here — and no record is not the same as no permission
        • Licensing → Get the licence and read its conditions
          • then → Does what's happening actually depart from the licence?
            • Yes — it's outside what the licence permits → Operating outside the licence
            • No, or the licence covers it → Not a licensing breach — so what is it?
      • No — this is a proposal, application or decision → Have you actually seen a physical statutory notice — lamppost, hoarding, shop window?
        • Yes — I'm standing in front of one → What colour is it?
          • Pale blue, A4 or larger → Take the details off the notice before you walk away
            • then → Is it marked as an application, a variation, or a review?
              • Review → This is the licensing story
              • Variation → Medium potential — worth one phone call, not a splash
              • New application → Find the hearing on the borough's committee calendar
                • then → Are there objections on the agenda — police, environmental health, or resident representations?
                  • Yes — representations have been lodged → (as above)
                  • No, or the papers aren't up yet → Routine for now — put it in the diary
          • White, yellow or something else → Take the reference number before you walk away
            • then → Look the reference up
              • then → Is it contentious?
                • Yes — one of those applies → This is the planning story
                • No — it's routine → Filler at best
        • No — I'm searching speculatively → Planning or licensing?
          • Planning → Set a postcode or ward alert
            • then → Anything in the list that looks large-scale or controversial?
              • Yes — something stands out → Cross-check the designations
                • then → (as above)
              • No — nothing obvious → Is there something already refused, where you are chasing the appeal?
                • Yes — chasing an appeal outcome → Search the appeal decisions
                  • then → (as above)
                • No → (as above)
          • Licensing → Crawl the boroughs one at a time
            • then → Is a sub-committee or hearing panel scheduled this week?
              • Yes → Are there objections on the agen… (as above)
              • No → (as above)

    Checked before this went on the page

    • Licensing Act 2003 site notices must legally be pale blue, A4 or larger, displayed for 28 consecutive days — a statutory requirement, not house style. Source
    • Planning site notices have no nationally mandated colour — white/yellow is common practice, not law — and the statutory minimum display/comment period is 21 days, though it varies by council and application type. Source
    • PlanningAlerts.app covers 9 of Greater Manchester's 10 boroughs — Salford is the one missing. No aggregator exists for licensing agendas; each borough's Modern.gov calendar has to be checked separately.
    • planningprecedent.co.uk's full-text appeal search is free with no login; planning.data.gov.uk is useful for verifying designations (listed building, conservation area, greenbelt) but does not track live applications, appeals, or licensing.
    • A breach of planning control is defined in s171A of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 as either carrying out development without the required permission, or failing to comply with a condition or limitation attached to a permission granted. Contravening the limits or conditions of permitted development rights under the GPDO 2015 is also a breach. Source — MHCLG planning practice guidance
    • Enforcement time limits (s171B, as amended by s115 of the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023, in force 25 April 2024, England only): 10 years where operational development was substantially completed on or after 25 April 2024, or an unauthorised change of use to a single dwellinghouse took place on or after that date; 4 years where either took place before it. Other breaches, including condition breaches, 10 years. Source
    • Licensing Act 2003 s8: each licensing authority must keep a register of premises licences, club premises certificates, personal licences and temporary event notices, must make the entries available for inspection by any person during office hours without payment, and must supply a copy of an entry on request — for which it may charge a reasonable fee. Source — legislation.gov.uk
