    The wizard hides the terrain. This is the terrain.

    • Have you seen something or told that something is wrong?
      • Yes — someone says the rules are being broken → Planning or licensing?
        • Planning → Find out what, if anything, was actually permitted
          • then → Compare the approved drawings and conditions with what's happening on the ground. What do you have?
            • A permission exists, and what's being built departs from it or conditions aren't being met → Check who's actually behind the site
              • then → Departure from an approved permission — the strongest version of this story
            • A permission exists, the building matches it and conditions have been met → The building matches — so read the conditions, not the drawings
            • Nothing on the portal at all → No digital shortcut exists here — and no record is not the same as no permission
        • Licensing → Get the licence and read its conditions
          • then → Could a Temporary Event Notice cover what you're seeing?
            • There's a TEN on record covering this date → Not a licensing breach — so what is it?
            • No TEN on record, or it doesn't cover this → Does what's happening actually depart from the licence?
              • Yes — it's outside what the licence permits → Check who's actually behind the premises
                • then → Operating outside the licence
              • No, or the licence covers it → (as above)
      • No — this is a proposal, application or decision → Have you actually seen a physical notice — lamppost, hoarding, shop window?
        • Yes — I'm standing in front of one → What colour is it?
          • Pale blue → Take the details off the notice before you walk away
            • then → Is it marked as an application, a variation, or a review?
              • Review → Check who's actually behind the premises
                • then → This could be an interesting licensing story
              • Variation → Medium potential — worth a bit more digging
              • New application → Find the hearing on the borough's committee calendar
                • then → Is a sub-committee or hearing panel scheduled this week?
                  • Yes → Are there objections on the agenda — police, environmental health, or resident representations?
                    • Yes — representations have been lodged → Check who's actually behind the… (as above)
                    • No, or the papers aren't up yet → Routine for now — keep digging for a little bit but the main action is to put it in the diary
                  • No → (as above)
          • White, yellow or something else → Take the reference number before you walk away
            • then → Look the reference up
              • then → Is it contentious?
                • Yes — one of those applies → Check who's actually behind the application
                  • then → This is the planning story
                • No — it's routine → Is there something refused or enforced against, where you are chasing the appeal?
                  • Yes — an application was refused and it's on appeal → Search the appeal decisions
                    • then → This is an appeal story — and the council no longer controls it
                  • Yes — an enforcement notice is being appealed → Search the appeal decisions (as above)
                  • No → Filler at best
        • I attended, or have seen coverage of, a committee or hearing where this was already decided → Planning or licensing?
          • Planning → Capture what actually happened at the meeting
            • then → What did the committee decide, and how did it get there?
              • Refused, or approved against the officer's recommendation → Check who's actually behind the… (as above)
              • Approved in line with the recommendation, little or no objection → Approved as recommended — a meeting happened, but nothing to write yet
              • Deferred, or decision delayed for more information → (as above)
          • Licensing → Capture what actually happened at the hearing
            • then → What did the committee decide?
              • Refused, or granted only with heavy new conditions, after evidence or objections → Check who's actually behind the… (as above)
              • Granted, with little or no opposition → Granted with little fuss — a meeting happened, but there's not much of a story yet
              • Deferred or adjourned to a future date → (as above)
        • No — I'm searching speculatively → Planning or licensing?
          • Planning → Set a postcode or ward alert

                • then → (as above)

    Useful resources

    • Licensing Act 2003 site notices must legally be pale blue, A4 or larger, displayed for 28 consecutive days — a statutory requirement, not house style. Source
    • Planning site notices have no nationally mandated colour — white/yellow is common practice, not law — and the statutory minimum display/comment period is 21 days, though it varies by council and application type. Source
    • PlanningAlerts.app covers 9 of Greater Manchester's 10 boroughs — Salford is the one missing. No aggregator exists for licensing agendas; each borough's Modern.gov calendar has to be checked separately.
    • planningprecedent.co.uk's full-text appeal search is free with no login; planning.data.gov.uk is useful for verifying designations (listed building, conservation area, greenbelt) but does not track live applications, appeals, or licensing.
    • A breach of planning control is defined in s171A of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 as either carrying out development without the required permission, or failing to comply with a condition or limitation attached to a permission granted. Contravening the limits or conditions of permitted development rights under the GPDO 2015 is also a breach. Source — MHCLG planning practice guidance
    • Enforcement time limits (s171B, as amended by s115 of the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023, in force 25 April 2024, England only): 10 years where operational development was substantially completed on or after 25 April 2024, or an unauthorised change of use to a single dwellinghouse took place on or after that date; 4 years where either took place before it. Other breaches, including condition breaches, 10 years. Source
    • Licensing Act 2003 s8: each licensing authority must keep a register of premises licences, club premises certificates, personal licences and temporary event notices, must make the entries available for inspection by any person during office hours without payment, and must supply a copy of an entry on request — for which it may charge a reasonable fee. Source — legislation.gov.uk
    • Temporary Event Notices (sections 100–110, Licensing Act 2003) let a premises trade outside its normal licence conditions for a short period without varying the licence. Source — Home Office guidance issued under section 182 of the Licensing Act 2003 (gov.uk)
    • Companies House's free company search (find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk) covers UK-registered companies only — sole traders and overseas entities won't appear. Source
    • For land owned by an overseas entity, beneficial ownership isn't shown in standard HM Land Registry title data — check the Register of Overseas Entities instead, introduced by the Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act 2022. Source
    • Planning appeal procedure (written representations, hearing, or inquiry) and third-party comment deadlines are set out in the Planning Inspectorate's current "Procedural Guide: planning appeals — England" on gov.uk — link to that page rather than stating a specific day-count, since these timescales have changed before. Source
